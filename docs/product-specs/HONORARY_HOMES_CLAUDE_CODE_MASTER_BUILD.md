## 2. Central operating idea

The entire website must make one loop understandable:

1. **LEARN** — begin with a course, orientation, college-aligned class, or pre-apprenticeship module.
2. **EARN** — enter paid employment or an approved apprenticeship pathway.
3. **BUILD** — develop skill through supervised work on real residential, commercial, and Vacant Bone projects.
4. **LIVE** — access independent housing support through Keyline where available and eligible.
5. **OWN** — prepare for a properly governed homeownership path through First Key or Quiet Key.
6. **MENTOR** — return as a crew leader, journey worker, instructor, property operator, or employer partner.

Call this system **The Honorary Loop**. It must appear as a signature interactive sequence, not a generic six-card row.

### Typography

Use open-source fonts that can be loaded through the framework’s font system or self-hosted.

- **Technical/navigation/records:** Roboto Condensed or a comparable condensed sans
- **Large statements/editorial:** Newsreader or a comparable serious, highly legible serif
- **Body/interface:** Inter or a comparable neutral sans

Rules:

- Hero statements use the serif, not a giant geometric sans.
- Property codes, measurements, timestamps, labels, statuses, and navigation use the condensed face.
- Body copy stays readable, restrained, and no smaller than 16px on mobile.
- Use sentence case for body content and uppercase only for labels, statuses, and navigation.
- Avoid excessive letter spacing in long headings.

### Grid and spacing

- Desktop: 12-column grid with a maximum content width around 1440px.
- Tablet: 8-column grid.
- Mobile: 4-column grid.
- Use a 4px base unit and a generous vertical rhythm.
- Major sections should feel like chapters, not stacked marketing bands.
- Use deliberate asymmetry: metadata can occupy 2–3 columns while a statement occupies 6–8.
- Let rules, coordinates, and record numbers extend across the grid to connect sections.
- Corners should be square or nearly square. Border radius should usually remain between 0 and 4px.

### Graphic language

Create a reusable visual grammar from:

- registration marks;
- thin survey lines;
- crop and measurement marks;
- lot and property coordinates;
- ledger rows;
- page folios;
- inspection stamps;
- document revision numbers;
- material tags;
- project codes;
- progress bars that resemble construction sequencing; and
- restrained topographic or parcel-line SVGs.

All graphics must be original code-native SVG or CSS unless an official asset is supplied. Decorative graphics should be `aria-hidden`; meaningful diagrams require accessible text equivalents.

### Signature graphics engine

Build a small, deterministic graphics engine rather than decorating pages with unrelated illustration. It should translate approved content into a family of living visual records.

Required graphic families:

1. **Structural sections** — simplified wall, floor, roof, stair, and opening assemblies that reveal the Honorary Loop in layers.
2. **Property traces** — parcel boundaries, footprints, measured lines, existing-condition hatching, and return-to-use overlays for Vacant Bone.
3. **Material registers** — honest swatch fields for timber, masonry, plaster, metal, insulation, paint, and reclaimed material, paired with source and project metadata.
4. **Route lines** — abstracted regional and pathway connections for Vermont, Maine, Baltimore, and State Line without publishing protected addresses.
5. **Competency marks** — precise progress notation for learned, observed, practiced, verified, and restricted tasks; never gamified badges.
6. **Evidence plates** — editorial charts, outcome records, source notes, dates, and verification states designed as publication pages rather than dashboard widgets.
7. **Revision fields** — issue numbers, drawing references, approval states, dates, and responsible owners that make the platform feel maintained.

Implementation rules:

- generate repeated linework with reusable TypeScript data and accessible SVG components;
- seed any visual variation so server and client render identically;
- bind color, stroke, typography, density, and motion to design tokens;
- expose a static fallback for every animated graphic;
- never encode meaning by color alone;
- provide text or table equivalents for meaningful graphics;
- export approved compositions to Open Graph, editorial-cover, capability-sheet, and social-image formats;
- use Canvas or WebGL only for one demonstrably valuable signature scene and only with static, reduced-motion, low-power, and no-JavaScript fallbacks; and
- never fabricate an architectural drawing, measurement, property boundary, or construction detail and present it as project truth.

The graphics engine must make raw operational information beautiful. It must not make decoration look like data.

### Image and film authorship

Treat media as commissioned editorial material, not content slots.

- Establish a shot list for every template before accepting photography.
- Mix territory, structure, hand, material, process, and completed-use frames.
- Use controlled shifts between 16:9 landscape, 4:5 portrait, 3:2 documentary, and extreme macro rather than cropping one image everywhere.
- Grade imagery toward natural Atlantic and northern light, mineral neutrals, honest shadow, restrained saturation, and visible material texture.
- Preserve skin texture, weather, dust, wear, unfinished conditions, and the signs of real work.
- Use film only when time and sequence add information; a still must carry the composition before the film loads.
- Every public image requires rights, consent, location/privacy review, focal point, alt-text decision, and expiration/review metadata.

Do not use AI-generated people, fake projects, or simulated before-and-after evidence as documentary material. Concept imagery, if ever used, must be unmistakably labeled as concept work and separated from project records.

Use documentary photography, not lifestyle advertising.

Preferred subjects:

- hands measuring, marking, fastening, repairing, finishing, or reviewing work;
- wide environmental views showing weather, site conditions, and place;
- material wear, old framing, repaired masonry, plaster, timber, steel, and water damage;
- occupied-looking but private-feeling finished spaces with no identifying resident information;
- learners and apprentices shown doing skilled work under appropriate supervision;
- commercial, residential, rural, small-city, and rowhouse contexts; and

## 14B. Anonymous backend AI staff

Build a private AI operations layer with the speed and breadth of a leading general-purpose assistant. It works only inside authenticated staff tools. It is not a public chatbot, does not have a public route, does not appear as a floating widget, and never converses directly with website visitors.

Do not copy Grok’s name, identity, prompts, proprietary behavior, or personality. Honorary Homes uses its own provider-agnostic systems, knowledge sources, permissions, and operating rules.

### Backend-only rule

- No public AI chat.
- No `/ask` page.
- No bot launcher.
- No AI avatar, character, employee profile, or public persona.
- No bot-authored message goes directly to a visitor, applicant, learner, client, partner, tenant, or housing participant.
- No autonomous publishing, emailing, texting, scheduling, payments, refunds, approvals, denials, offers, estimates, contracts, or record changes.
- All externally visible text or action produced with AI assistance is reviewed and deliberately approved by an authorized person before release.

The public website presents Honorary Homes, its people, services, records, and forms. The AI layer remains an internal productivity and quality system.

### Anonymous, role-based operation

The bots have service roles rather than personal identities.

- Use internal identifiers such as `publisher_assistant`, `claim_sentinel`, `intake_triage`, `course_navigator`, and `registry_assistant`.
- Do not give a bot a human name, headshot, résumé, biography, signature, voice clone, or fictional lived experience.
- Do not attribute AI-produced drafts to a named human before that person reviews and adopts the work.
- External communications may use an approved team signature such as `Honorary Homes Team` only after a responsible person reviews and sends them.
- Internal audit logs record which service role, model configuration, staff operator, source records, and approval event were involved.
- Individual staff names do not need to appear publicly unless a real person chooses or is required to be the responsible contact.

The operating principle is **anonymous in identity, private in operation, accountable in audit**.

### Human authority and decision boundaries

Backend AI may draft, summarize, categorize, compare, flag, and recommend. It may not make consequential decisions.

It may not:

- approve or reject construction leads;
- issue a binding estimate, contract, schedule, or change order;
- decide apprenticeship selection, employment eligibility, wages, discipline, or accommodation;
- decide housing eligibility, tenant screening, priority, rent, credit, ownership readiness, or mortgage qualification;
- evaluate a survivor narrative;
- provide safety planning, therapy, victim advocacy, crisis counseling, legal advice, mortgage advice, engineering advice, or medical advice;
- promise placement, credentials, housing, financing, or property acquisition;
- expose protected addresses, private underwriting, applicant data, partner data, or staff-only records;
- publish editorial, sales, course, property, or partnership content;
- send a message or submit a form;
- issue or reverse a payment;
- create an appointment; or
- modify an authoritative record without a human-confirmed workflow.

Named teams and governed processes remain responsible for all high-impact decisions.

### Data boundaries

- AI systems do not watch visitors browse, read form fields as they are typed, or ingest abandoned form content.
- Quiet Key and survivor-related data never enters the general AI layer.
- Housing records remain outside the general intake and publishing indexes.
- Raw payment data never enters an AI prompt or log.
- Applicant and employee information is minimized and separated from editorial, marketing, and course data.
- Private prompts, outputs, and source records are excluded from public analytics.
- Model providers may not use Honorary Homes data for training unless a separately reviewed contract and policy explicitly permit it.
- Retention, deletion, access, and incident procedures are documented.
- Privacy and legal notices accurately describe automated processing where applicable; do not make deceptive or legally false privacy statements.

### Approval pattern

Every AI-assisted external output uses this sequence:

1. authorized staff requests a draft or analysis;
2. the system records the permitted source set;
3. the bot produces a clearly marked internal draft;
4. automated checks flag missing sources, private data, expired claims, and risky language;
5. a responsible person edits and approves or rejects it;
6. a separate publish/send action records the human approval; and
7. the audit log retains the minimum metadata needed for accountability.

Removing an “AI draft” label inside the private workspace does not publish the item. Publication always requires a distinct human action.

## 29. Error, empty, and success states
